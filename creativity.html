01 / UNDERSTANDING THE CONCEPT 01—09

Creativity is more
than making things
look beautiful.

Marketing creativity is the process of developing original, relevant and meaningful ideas that help businesses communicate value and connect with people.

It involves combining imagination with marketing knowledge to develop messages, experiences and campaigns that attract attention, communicate benefits and create memorable brand experiences.

A creative marketer does not simply ask, "How can I advertise this product?" The better question is: "How can I make people understand why this product matters to them?"

THE CENTRAL IDEA Creativity gives marketing its expression. Strategy gives that expression its direction.
02 / THE FOUNDATIONS SIX PILLARS

Six pillars of
marketing creativity.

Different creative disciplines work together to shape how a business presents itself, communicates and builds relationships with consumers.

01
◈
IDENTITY & POSITIONING

Brand Identity

Creating a distinctive identity through brand names, logos, colours, personality, positioning and consistent communication.

Marketing application

A brand should be recognisable and communicate a clear promise. Its identity helps consumers distinguish it from competing alternatives.

Example

A Nigerian fashion business can develop a recognisable identity through its visual style, packaging, tone of voice and customer experience.

02
z
PERSUASION & COMMUNICATION

Advertising Creativity

Developing attention-grabbing advertisements that communicate product benefits and encourage consumers to consider a brand.

Marketing application

Advertisements combine headlines, visuals, storytelling and calls to action to communicate a persuasive message.

Example

An advert for a delivery service could focus on convenience and time saved rather than simply listing delivery prices.

03
◎
EMOTION & CONNECTION

Brand Storytelling

Using meaningful stories and experiences to communicate a brand's purpose, values and relationship with its audience.

Marketing application

Storytelling gives audiences a context they can understand and relate to, instead of presenting only product features.

Example

A small business can tell the story of how its products are made and the people behind the business.

04
▧
CONTENT & ENGAGEMENT

Content Creativity

Creating valuable and engaging content that educates, entertains, informs or inspires a particular audience.

Marketing application

Content can take different forms including articles, short videos, infographics, podcasts and social media posts.

Example

A skincare brand could publish educational content explaining skincare routines instead of relying exclusively on promotional posts.

05
◇
DESIGN & PERCEPTION

Visual Communication

Using colour, typography, imagery, composition and visual hierarchy to make marketing messages easier to understand.

Marketing application

Good visual communication directs attention and helps audiences identify the most important parts of a message.

Example

A product poster can use a clear headline, strong product image and visible call to action to communicate effectively.

06
@
INTEGRATED COMMUNICATION

Creative Campaigns

Bringing multiple creative ideas and communication channels together around one consistent marketing objective.

Marketing application

A campaign can coordinate social media, outdoor advertising, public relations, promotional activities and other channels.

Example

A new product launch could use a teaser campaign, an event, influencer content and follow-up customer communication.

03 / FROM THOUGHT TO EXECUTION THE PROCESS

How a marketing idea
becomes a campaign.

Creativity is not always a random moment of inspiration. In marketing, it can follow a structured process supported by research, planning and evaluation.

01
DISCOVERY

Research the market

Study the target audience, competitors, market conditions and customer needs. Understand the problem before developing an idea.

KEY QUESTION: WHO ARE WE TRYING TO REACH?
02
UNDERSTANDING

Identify the problem

Define the marketing challenge. Is the business trying to increase awareness, improve perception, introduce a product or encourage trial?

KEY QUESTION: WHAT NEEDS TO CHANGE?
03
IMAGINATION

Generate creative ideas

Brainstorm possible concepts, messages, campaign themes and different ways to reach the intended audience.

KEY QUESTION: WHAT IS A BETTER WAY TO COMMUNICATE?
04
STRATEGY

Develop the concept

Select an idea that is relevant to the audience and consistent with the brand's objectives. Develop the message and creative direction.

KEY QUESTION: WHY SHOULD THIS IDEA WORK?
05
EXECUTION

Create the campaign

Produce the creative materials, choose suitable communication channels and deliver a consistent message to the target audience.

KEY QUESTION: HOW WILL PEOPLE EXPERIENCE IT?
06
EVALUATION

Measure the outcome

Evaluate performance using appropriate measures such as reach, engagement, brand awareness, enquiries and conversions.

KEY QUESTION: DID WE ACHIEVE OUR OBJECTIVE?

06 / CAMPAIGN INSPIRATION GLOBAL & AFRICAN

Ideas that made
brands memorable.

Great marketing campaigns offer lessons in positioning, storytelling, emotional connection and creative communication. Here are examples worth studying.

GLOBAL / BRAND STORYTELLING

Coca-Cola — Share a Coke

The personalised-name approach made product packaging part of a social experience, encouraging people to share and connect.

MARKETING LESSON

Personalisation can make a familiar product feel more personally relevant.

GLOBAL / BRAND POSITIONING

Nike — Just Do It

Nike's enduring slogan connects the brand with action, determination and athletic aspiration rather than focusing only on product features.

MARKETING LESSON

A powerful brand message can communicate an identity and inspire an audience.

NIGERIA / CULTURAL POSITIONING

Guinness — Made of More

Guinness has used the Made of More platform to communicate individuality, character and distinctive brand values.

MARKETING LESSON

Brands can use a consistent idea to build a recognisable personality.

AFRICA / CULTURAL CONNECTION

MTN — Y'ello

MTN's recognisable Y'ello identity demonstrates how distinctive brand language and colour can support recognition across markets.

MARKETING LESSON

Consistent brand assets help audiences identify and remember a brand.
